Why do almost all brain medicines completely fail?
Inside your skull sits a locked wall of endothelial cells.
Scientists call this cellular fortress the blood brain barrier.
Special protein seams called tight junctions weld every gap shut.
Toxins, bacteria, and huge molecules slam straight into this shield.
Over ninety percent of small drugs cannot cross this wall.
Only tiny molecules or special nutrient transporters earn a pass.
That microscopic lock keeps your brain alive every second.
